Meet Peter — the AI Employee who owns your website.

Most businesses face two choices when the website falls behind: hire a web developer or buy another platform. We built a third option.

By Spencer Jones · August 2, 2026

← Back to the YourOS Blog

Every business owner I talk to has the same website story.

They know the site needs attention. A service page that's been wrong for eight months. A specials section that still shows last spring's offer. A contact form nobody's updated since the old manager left. They know it. But between phones and customers and quotes and payroll, the website stays exactly as it is.

When they finally decide to fix it, they look at two doors.

Door one: Hire a web developer or a marketing person. A real human employee. Starting salary, benefits, onboarding time, and the full cost of employment — usually $40,000 to $70,000 a year by the time you add it up. Even a part-time contractor is $2,000 to $5,000 a month for ongoing work.

Door two: Buy another platform. A website builder, a CMS, a page editor, a marketing suite. Six to twelve months before it's configured correctly. Training for someone on your team. And you're still the one who has to actually do the work — the platform doesn't do it for you.

There is a third door.

We built it because neither of those options made sense for a small or mid-size business that just needs the website job to have an owner.

Peter is that owner.

Peter is a YourOS AI Employee. His job is your website. Send him a note — an email, a voice message, a photo of something you want to match — and Peter builds it. He sends a demo before anything goes live. You approve, request changes, or redirect him. The work gets done.

You don't manage a contractor timeline. You don't log into a platform. You don't explain what you need to a sales rep who then routes you to a support team. You send an email. Peter handles it.

What Peter actually does.

Here's what a typical week looks like for a business that has Peter on staff:

  • Monday: Owner emails Peter, "Can you add a section to our homepage about the new service we're offering?" Peter builds it, sends a screenshot for approval by Tuesday morning.
  • Wednesday: Owner replies with one tweak. Peter makes it. Done.
  • Friday: Owner notices a phone number is wrong on the contact page. Forwards the email to Peter. Fixed in an hour.

No scheduling calls. No ticket systems. No "we'll get to it next sprint." Peter treats each instruction like a job from his employer — because that's what it is.

The things Peter can build: new service pages, landing pages for campaigns, blog posts, location pages, content updates, offer pages, lead magnets, seasonal promotions, SEO-focused pages, and general site maintenance.

What Peter is not.

Peter is not a software subscription. You don't get a login and a blank canvas. You get an employee — someone who owns a defined job and produces output you review before it goes live.

Peter is also not a contractor you manage. You're not his client. You're his employer. That distinction matters because it changes how the relationship works. You set direction. Peter produces deliverables and asks clarifying questions. You don't chase him for status updates.
